Barnaby was not an ambitious caterpillar. While his siblings spent their days in a frantic, leaf-munching marathon, Barnaby preferred the philosophical approach. He would spend hours perched on the edge of a juicy oak leaf, gazing up at the vast, sun-drenched canopy, wondering what lay beyond the garden fence.

"Stop daydreaming, Barnaby! Eat your greens!" rasped Martha, a notoriously grumpy slug who lived beneath his favorite branch.

"I am eating, Martha. Metaphorically," Barnaby replied, taking a slow, contemplative nibble.

Unlike the others who wanted only to grow fat and heavy, Barnaby felt a strange, persistent itch beneath his skin. It wasn't a spiritual calling, however. It was a sudden, violent stomach ache.

Before he could ponder the cosmos any further, Barnaby’s tummy let out a noise like a troubled foghorn. The single, tiny nibble of oak leaf hit a weird patch of fermenting sap inside him, sparking an instantaneous chemical chain reaction. Barnaby didn't wrap himself in silk; he began to expand. His skin stretched like a over-inflated balloon, swelling from the size of a thimble to the size of a fully grown grapefruit in under ten seconds.

"Good grief!" Martha yelped, diving under a rotting leaf as Barnaby’s gargantuan body crushed three twigs. "What did you eat?!"

Barnaby groaned, now the size of a bowling ball and trapped on a branch that was bending dangerously low. Every deep thought in his head was immediately replaced by a desperate need to vent pressurized air.

PFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFRRRRRT.

A jet of trapped gas erupted from his rear spiracles with the force of a leaf blower. The sheer thrust blasted Barnaby off the oak branch entirely. He didn't spin a chrysalis; he became a runaway, squeaking projectile, zooming wildly through the air like an untied party balloon. He ricocheted off the greenhouse glass, whistled past a startled robin, and zipped straight over the garden fence he had spent years longing to cross.

With one final, thunderous POP, the pressure equalized, deflating Barnaby back to a sensible size just as he drifted gracefully down onto a patch of soft clover on the other side.

Martha peeked through a gap in the fence, her mouth agape. "Too much philosophy, if you ask me."

Barnaby dusted off his tiny feet, looked back at the garden he'd just conquered in three seconds flat, and chuckled. He didn't need wings to reach the horizon—just a little compressed air. He took a deep breath and decided to take a walk in the forest.
