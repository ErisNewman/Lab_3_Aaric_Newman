Barnaby was not an ambitious caterpillar. While his siblings spent their days in a frantic, leaf-munching marathon, Barnaby preferred the philosophical approach. He would spend hours perched on the edge of a juicy oak leaf, gazing up at the vast, sun-drenched canopy, wondering what lay beyond the garden fence.

"Stop daydreaming, Barnaby! Eat your greens!" rasped Martha, a notoriously grumpy slug who lived beneath his favorite branch.

"I am eating, Martha. Metaphorically," Barnaby replied, taking a slow, contemplative nibble.

Unlike the others who wanted only to grow fat and heavy, Barnaby felt a strange, persistent itch beneath his skin. It wasn't hunger. It felt like a song he couldn't quite remember, or a pair of invisible wings whispering secrets into his spiracles.

One crisp autumn morning, the itch became unbearable. His skin grew tight, and an overwhelming drowsiness settled into his tiny joints. Remembering the ancient wisdom passed down through generations of caterpillars, Barnaby spun himself a snug, silken sleeping bag.

Inside, the world went completely dark. It was quiet, save for the rhythmic beating of a heart he didn't quite recognize. He dissolved, letting go of everything he thought he knew about being a caterpillar—the crawl, the mud, the endless chewing.

Weeks blurred into a dream of infinite blue skies and dancing breezes.

When he finally woke, the shell cracked. Barnaby pushed through, tumbling into the blinding sunlight. He felt impossibly light. He stretched out a pair of magnificent, sapphire-blue wings, painted with intricate patterns of gold.

Martha the slug slid into view, squinting upward. "Well, I'll be," she muttered. "Too much philosophy, if you ask me."

Barnaby didn't reply. With a single, effortless flutter, he lifted off the branch. He wasn't crawling anymore. He was flying, finally catching up to the horizon he had spent his whole life dreaming about.
